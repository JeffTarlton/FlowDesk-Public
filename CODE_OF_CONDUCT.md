# Code of Conduct

FlowDesk is built by people with different backgrounds, experience levels and first languages. We want everyone who uses, discusses or contributes to the project to feel welcome and safe. This document explains what we expect and what happens when those expectations are not met.

## Our commitment

Maintainers and contributors commit to making participation in FlowDesk a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body, disability, ethnicity, gender identity or expression, level of experience, education, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, sexual identity or orientation, or socio-economic status.

## What we expect

- **Be kind and patient.** Assume good intent, and remember that many contributors are volunteers and some are new to open source.
- **Be constructive.** Critique ideas and code, not people. Explain the reasoning behind a review comment, and suggest an alternative where you can.
- **Be inclusive.** Use clear language, avoid jargon when a simpler word works, and help newcomers find their way.
- **Respect people's time and boundaries.** Keep discussions on topic, accept a "no" gracefully, and do not repeatedly ping people for a response.
- **Protect privacy.** Never share someone else's personal information, and never post real customer data, credentials or private project details in issues or pull requests.
- **Take responsibility.** If you hurt someone, even unintentionally, listen, apologize and do better.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, intimidation, stalking or threats of any kind.
- Insults, slurs, or derogatory comments about a person or group.
- Sexualized language or imagery, and unwelcome attention of any kind.
- Personal or political attacks, trolling, and deliberately derailing discussions.
- Publishing someone's private information (such as an address or email) without their explicit permission.
- Spam, including repeated self-promotion that is unrelated to the discussion.
- Encouraging or advocating any of the above.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies in all FlowDesk project spaces, including the GitHub repository, issues, pull requests, discussions and reviews. It also applies when someone represents the project elsewhere, for example when posting on behalf of FlowDesk.

## Reporting a problem

If you experience or witness behavior that breaks this Code of Conduct, please tell the maintainers privately. You do not need to be sure that it is a violation before reporting it.

- **On GitHub content** (an issue, pull request or comment): open its **...** menu, choose **Report content**, and then **Report to repository admins**, if that option is available. You can also choose **Report abuse to GitHub Support** to involve GitHub directly. GitHub describes both options in [Reporting abuse or spam](https://docs.github.com/en/communities/maintaining-your-safety-on-github/reporting-abuse-or-spam).
- **Anything else**, or if the option above is not available: use the repository's private reporting form. Open the **Security** tab, click **Report a vulnerability**, and start the title with "Code of Conduct:". Only the repository maintainers can read these reports.

Please include what happened, where (links help), when, and who was involved. Reports are handled confidentially. We will not share the reporter's identity without permission, and we do not tolerate retaliation against anyone who reports in good faith.

If a maintainer is involved in the incident, they will not take part in handling the report.

## How we respond

Maintainers will review every report, may ask for more information, and will decide on an action that fits the situation. Possible outcomes, roughly in order of severity:

1. **Private note.** A reminder of these guidelines and an explanation of why the behavior was a problem.
2. **Warning.** A clear warning, with consequences for further violations. Offending content may be edited, hidden or removed.
3. **Temporary ban.** A time-limited block from interacting with the project.
4. **Permanent ban.** For serious or repeated violations, a permanent block from the project's spaces.

Maintainers may remove, edit or reject comments, commits, code, issues and other contributions that do not follow this Code of Conduct.
